# CV & Resume Best Practices (Nigerian Context)

## Structure
A strong CV for the Nigerian job market should include: Contact Info, Professional Summary (2-3 lines), Skills, Work Experience (reverse chronological), Education, Certifications, and Projects (especially for tech roles). Keep it to 1-2 pages maximum for early-career candidates.

## Common Mistakes
Many Nigerian graduates include a full "Personal Data" section (age, marital status, state of origin, religion) which is outdated for most modern employers, especially international and remote-first companies. Avoid generic objective statements like "seeking a challenging role to utilize my skills" — replace with specific, role-targeted summaries.

## Tech Roles Specifically
For software development, data, and AI/ML roles, a "Projects" section is often more valuable than work experience for entry-level candidates. Link to GitHub repositories and live deployed demos (e.g. Streamlit apps, portfolio sites). Quantify impact where possible: "Built and deployed an ML model achieving 87% accuracy" is stronger than "Worked on machine learning project."

## ATS Optimization
Many companies use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to filter CVs before a human sees them. Use standard section headers (not creative ones), avoid tables/columns that break parsing, and mirror keywords from the job description naturally into your CV.

## Remote & International Applications
For remote roles with international companies, emphasize async communication skills, self-direction, and specific tools (Slack, Notion, Git/GitHub, Jira). Include your timezone (WAT, UTC+1) and English proficiency if relevant.
