# Nigerian Tech Job Market Overview

## In-Demand Roles (2025-2026)
Frontend/Fullstack Developers (React, Next.js), Data Analysts, AI/ML Engineers, DevOps/Cloud Engineers, Product Designers (UI/UX), and Backend Developers (Node.js, Python/Django) remain consistently in demand across Nigerian tech hubs (Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt) and for remote-first companies hiring Nigerian talent.

## Salary Expectations (Entry-Level, Local Market)
Entry-level developers at Nigerian startups typically earn between NGN 150,000 - 400,000/month depending on city and company stage. Remote roles for international companies (paid in USD/GBP) often pay significantly more, sometimes 3-5x local rates, but are more competitive and usually require a strong portfolio or prior experience.

## Where to Look
Popular platforms for Nigerian tech jobs include LinkedIn, AngelList/Wellfound (for startups), Jobberman, and tech-specific communities like the Nigerian developer Twitter/X community, Discord servers for bootcamps, and Andela's talent network. Referrals remain one of the strongest channels — building a public presence (GitHub, portfolio, content) helps generate inbound opportunities.

## Bootcamp Graduates
Graduates from bootcamps (like AI/ML or coding bootcamps) benefit most from having 2-3 deployed, well-documented projects over a long list of tutorials completed. Employers in Nigeria's tech scene increasingly value demonstrated project work and GitHub activity over certificates alone. Building in public (documenting the learning journey on platforms like Instagram, X, or LinkedIn) can also open doors through community visibility.

## Common Entry Points
Many Nigerian developers enter the industry through: internships at local startups, freelance/contract work (Upwork, local gigs), open-source contributions, or junior roles at agencies before moving to product companies. Government and NGO-backed programs (e.g. 3MTT - 3 Million Technical Talent) have also become a pathway for skill certification.
